Our Mission

Make service visible. Keep every story alive.

Dog Tag Day honors veterans, active-duty service members, military families, and every life represented by military dog tags.

Some stories are joyful. Some are painful. Some remain private. No one is required to tell a story before they are ready. Wearing the tags is enough.

This is not about celebrating war. It is about recognizing service, preserving memory, and reminding people that they are seen.

Official Annual Observance

April 18

One day each year for Americans to wear their dog tags, share stories by choice, honor those who served, and remember those who never came home.

“Where did you serve?”

Take Part

Three simple actions can start a national tradition.

01

Wear Your Tags

Veterans and active-duty service members are invited to wear their military dog tags visibly on April 18.

02

Tell Your Story

Share only what feels right: where you served, whom you remember, or what the tags mean to you.

03

Honor Ours

Supporters can wear a white shirt, recognize someone wearing tags, listen respectfully, and help preserve a story.

Stories & Tributes

Behind every set of tags is a life worth remembering.

Phase 2 opens the door to veteran stories, military-family tributes, and respectful memorials. Nothing will be published without the contributor’s permission.

Dog Tag Day Foundation

Born in Las Vegas

Dog Tag Day begins in a city seen by millions of residents and visitors, with a vision to grow into an annual tradition across the nation.

One nation. One mission. One day.

The Dog Tag Day Pledge

“Every April 18, I will wear my dog tags or stand beside those who do. I will recognize service with dignity, listen without judgment, and help ensure that no story is forgotten and no person feels unseen.”
