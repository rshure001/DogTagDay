Why Dog Tag Day Exists

ONCE THE UNIFORM COMES OFF,

For more than sixty years, I celebrated Veterans Day—yet rarely knew who around me had served.

Dog Tag Day changes that.

Every April 18, put on the tags. Let America see the person, hear the story, and remember the service.

Exactly What Happens on Dog Tag Day

What do I physically do on April 18?

Participate wherever you are. No ticket, meeting, or special location is required.

1

Wear the tags

If you served, put on or display your military dog tags.

2

Honor someone

Family members may wear or display the tags of someone they love or remember.

3

Acknowledge them

If you see dog tags, recognize the person — a nod, handshake, or simply, “I see you.”

4

Share the story

Take a photo or video, tell the story behind the tags, and invite others to do the same.

Our Mission

Don’t let our veterans fade into the background.

Dog Tag Day is a national movement built around one simple act of visibility. Veterans and service members are invited to wear or display their dog tags. Families may wear tags in honor or memory of someone they love. Those stories deserve to be seen, heard, and remembered.

April 18

One day. One visible sign.

If you served

Put on your dog tags. They say, “I served.”

If you see the tags

Acknowledge the person wearing them — a nod, handshake, or simply, “I see you.”

If you are family

Wear or display the tags of someone you love and help keep their story alive.

The People Behind the Tags

Help America see who served.

A veteran may live two doors away and still feel invisible. A family may hold a set of tags—and a story no one has ever asked to hear. A service member may come home, remove the uniform, and watch the sacrifice disappear from public view.

Dog Tag Day changes that. On April 18, the tags become visible—and so do the lives, courage, grief, pride, and families behind them.

We need sponsors, partners, and funders who will help carry that recognition into communities across America. Your support helps preserve stories, reach families, equip local organizers, and build a national moment that tells every veteran: You are seen. You are remembered. You still matter.

Don’t sponsor another date on the calendar. Help build the day America looks its veterans in the eye.

A Message from the Founder
“For too long, too many who served have moved quietly through the world—unseen, their stories carried silently within them.

Dog tags are more than metal. They carry a name, a life, a promise, and the memory of those who stood beside us.

I founded Dog Tag Day so that every April 18, we bring those stories into the light. Put them on. Let America see them. Let every veteran know: you are seen, you are remembered, and you will never fade into the background.”
RONNIE SHURE — FOUNDER & PRESIDENT, DOG TAG DAY FOUNDATION
