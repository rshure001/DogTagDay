Our Mission

Preserving service, sacrifice, and legacy.

Dog Tag Day honors service, stories, identity, brotherhood, and honor every April 18.

It gives veterans, active-duty service members, military families, schools, businesses, and communities a visible way to recognize service and keep stories alive.

April 18 — What Do I Do?

If you served: Put on your dog tags. “I served.”

If you see the tags: Acknowledge the person wearing them. “I see you.”

Share the story: With permission, take a photo or short video and share the story behind the tags.

Stories Behind the Tags

Every set of dog tags tells a story.

Lena Pendleton

Daughter of a Veteran

I would like to honor and remember my Dad. A man who meant so much to so many and whose life left a lasting mark on everyone fortunate enough to know him.

My Dad proudly served in the United States Air Force. His service was a reflection of the kind of man he was: dedicated, courageous, dependable and willing to put others before himself. He carried that same sense of duty and strength into every part of his life.

He was the person whose presence helped shape who I am, whose lessons I will carry with me for the rest of my life. I will remember the things he taught me—not only through his words, but through the way he lived his life.

Dad, you served your country with honor. You loved your family with all your heart and you will never be forgotten.

Continue to rest peaceful, Dad.
Until we meet again.
Always, your little girl,
Lena Joyce
