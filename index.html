Year-Round Veteran Support • Program in Development

We don’t just recognize veterans. We help them take the next step.

A veteran may need help with benefits, food, housing, transportation, employment, home repairs, legal assistance, or simply finding the right organization to call. Dog Tag Day Foundation is building a year-round support program designed to make that process easier.

“The lower I go, the more I mean it when I pray.”

Sometimes helping starts by sitting down, listening, and staying with someone through the hard part.

1. We listen.

We start with the veteran—not a form. What do they need? What have they already tried? What is getting in the way?

2. We find the right resource.

We identify established veteran, nonprofit, government, and community services that fit the need.

3. We help make the connection.

We help the veteran understand the next step and where to go.

4. We follow up.

We check back. Did they reach someone? Was the problem solved? If not, we help identify another path.

The goal is simple: no veteran should have to navigate everything alone.
Veteran Listening • Suicide Prevention Awareness

You Are Not Alone.

Sometimes the first step is having someone who will listen. You served. You matter. Dog Tag Day believes veterans and their families deserve to be seen, heard, and connected to support without shame or judgment.

We listen.

We make space for veterans to share their stories, concerns, or need for connection with dignity and respect.

We connect.

We help veterans and families find established veteran services, peer support, and qualified professional resources.

We help prevent suicide.

We raise awareness, share trusted crisis resources, and encourage reaching out before a crisis becomes an emergency.

Need to talk right now?

Veterans Crisis Line

Free, confidential support is available 24/7 to veterans, service members, and people who care about them.

Call 988, then press 1

Or text 838255 or chat online.

If someone is in immediate danger, call 911. Dog Tag Day offers community listening and connections to resources; it is not a crisis line or a substitute for professional care.
