Our Mission

Preserving service, sacrifice, and legacy.

Dog Tag Day honors service, stories, identity, brotherhood, and honor every April 18.

It gives veterans, active-duty service members, military families, schools, businesses, and communities a visible way to recognize service and keep stories alive.

Seen. Heard. Remembered.

Official Observance

APRIL
18

April 18 — What Do I Do?

If you served: Put on your dog tags. “I served.”

If you see the tags: Acknowledge the person wearing them with a nod, handshake, conversation, or fist to the heart. “I see you.”

Share the story: With permission, take a photo or short video and share the story behind the tags.

A Shared Connection

When you see the tags, you know.

We may have served in different places, different units, and different eras. The tags represent a shared beginning and a shared understanding.

Different paths. Shared beginning. The tags connect us.

Participation is always voluntary.

Wear your tags with pride. If talking feels right, share your story. If not, a simple nod or smile is welcome. Your service stands on its own.

Why We Wear Them

Identity. Brotherhood. Honor.

Identity

Dog tags connect a service member to a name, a history, and a life of service.

Brotherhood

They recognize the bond created through training, duty, sacrifice, and shared experience.

Honor

They remind us to recognize service respectfully and preserve the stories behind the names.

Stories Behind the Tags

Every set of dog tags tells a story.

Community stories are posted publicly when submitted. Dog Tag Day may remove content afterward when necessary.

Stories Shared With Dog Tag Day

In Memory of James Calhoun Houston

Name: Lena Pendleton Connection: Daughter of a Veteran I would like to honor and remember my Dad. A man who meant so much to so many and whose life left a lasting mark on everyone fortunate enough to know him. My Dad proudly served in the United States Air Force. His service was a reflection of the kind of man he was: dedicated, courageous, dependable and willing to put others before himself. He carried that same sense of duty and strength into every part of his life. He was the person whose presence helped shape who I am, whose lessons I will carry with me for the rest of my life. I will remember the things he taught me—not only through his words, but through the way he lived his life. Dad, you served your country with honor. You loved your family with all your heart and you will never be forgotten. Continue to rest peaceful, Dad. Until we meet again. Always, your little girl, Lena Joyce

A salute to everyone who served

Name: Willie Beard III Connection: Family I want to salute everyone that has ever served in the Military. The sacrifice everyone of you made is priceless. I have family currently in the Navy and a brother from another mother who went to Iraq and Afghanistan. They made it home; a lot of people did not. May Jesus bless everyone that is currently serving our Country USA. Love Willie Beard III

With gratitude for those who served

Name: Willie Beard III Connection: Family To all the men and women who have served in the United States military, thank you for your immeasurable sacrifices. I am deeply proud of my nephew currently serving in the Navy, and a close friend who bravely served in Iraq and Afghanistan. While I am thankful for their safe return, I honor and remember those who made the ultimate sacrifice. May God bless all who are actively serving our nation today. Respectfully, Willie Beard III

A Message from the Founder

“I founded Dog Tag Day because every set of military dog tags represents a life of service, sacrifice, courage, and commitment. Those stories deserve to be seen, heard, and remembered.”
RONNIE SHURE — FOUNDER & PRESIDENT, DOG TAG DAY FOUNDATION

Dog Tag Day complements existing traditions of military recognition and remembrance. It does not replace Veterans Day or require anyone to speak publicly about their service.

Get Involved

Help build Dog Tag Day in your community.
