Our Mission

Don’t let our veterans fade into the background.

Dog Tag Day is a national movement built around one simple act of visibility. On April 18, veterans and service members are invited to wear or display their dog tags so America can see the people behind the service. Families may wear tags in honor or memory of someone they love.

Veterans Day and Memorial Day are sacred American observances. Dog Tag Day does not replace them. April 18 gives us another way to recognize the people who served this country and carried the responsibilities of military service.

Identity • Brotherhood • Honor. Those stories deserve to be seen, heard, and remembered.

One Day. One Visible Sign.

April 18 — What Do I Do?

If you served: Put on your dog tags. They say, “I served.”

If you see the tags: Acknowledge the person wearing them — a nod, handshake, fist to the heart, or simply, “I see you.”

If you are family: Wear or display the tags of someone you love and help keep their story alive.

Take a picture or video. Share the person, the tags, and the story behind them.

What the Tags Mean

More than metal. A life of service.

Dog tags are one of the most recognizable symbols of military identity. On Dog Tag Day, they become a visible connection between those who served and the communities they served.

America Can Join In

April 18 belongs in every community.

The goal is simple: make service visible, invite stories, and acknowledge the people behind the uniform.

Veterans & Families

Wear the tags, honor someone you love, share a photograph, or tell the story behind the tags.

Schools & Communities

Invite veterans to be seen and heard. Create space for students and neighbors to listen to their stories.

Businesses & Organizations

Recognize April 18, encourage participation, display the message, and help veterans know their service is remembered.
