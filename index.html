Our Mission

Make service visible. Keep memory alive.

Dog Tag Day honors veterans, active-duty service members, military families, and those remembered through military dog tags.

Each April 18, people are invited to wear their tags, recognize others who do, and share stories only when they choose. Wearing the tags is enough.

This movement is not about celebrating war. It is about dignity, recognition, service, sacrifice, and human connection.

01

Honor

Recognize those who served, those serving now, their families, and those who never came home.

02

Share

Preserve first-person stories, family memories, and the history carried by every set of tags.

03

Unite

Build a respectful tradition that brings veterans, families, supporters, schools, and communities together.

04

Inspire

Turn one simple symbol into conversations that teach, connect, and prevent stories from being forgotten.

Official Annual Observance

April 18

Wear a white T-shirt. Wear your military dog tags. When you see someone else wearing theirs, offer a wave, a nod, a handshake, or a respectful question:

“Where did you serve?”

Participate

Four simple ways to stand with Dog Tag Day.

1

Wear Your Tags

Wear your own tags, a loved one’s tags with permission, or stand beside those who wear theirs.

2

Wear White

A plain white T-shirt creates a visible symbol of unity without replacing the meaning of the tags.

3

Recognize Someone

Offer a wave, nod, handshake, or a sincere “Thank you for your service.”

4

Tell the Story

Share only what feels right. Listen without judgment. Respect silence and privacy.

From the Founder

“I wear my dog tags every day. After living in Las Vegas for 25 years, I realized I almost never saw another person wearing theirs. I wanted to create a day when service becomes visible—when one set of tags can open a respectful conversation, preserve a memory, and remind someone that they are seen.”

Ronnie Shure
Founder, CEO & President
