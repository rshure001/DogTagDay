Our mission

Make military service visible, personal, and remembered.

Dog tags carry names, memories, sacrifice, and service. On April 18, we encourage people to wear them where they can be seen, begin conversations, and preserve the stories connected to military service.

01

Honor service

Recognize veterans, active-duty personnel, and military families for their courage and sacrifice.

02

Share stories

Invite meaningful conversations about where people served, what they experienced, and what their service means.

03

Build connection

Create a nationwide tradition that brings communities together through remembrance, gratitude, and respect.

How to participate

Three simple steps for April 18.

  1. 1

    Wear dog tags

    Wear your own tags, a loved one’s tags with permission, or a respectful commemorative set.

  2. 2

    Start a conversation

    Ask: “Where did you serve?” or “What story do your dog tags carry?”

  3. 3

    Share the message

    Post a photo or story using #DogTagDay and invite others to take part.

“I have worn my dog tags every day. Dog Tag Day began with one simple belief: the stories behind those tags should be seen, heard, and remembered.”

— Ronnie Shure, Founder, CEO & President

Our story

Born in Las Vegas. Built for the nation.

After decades in Las Vegas, Ronnie Shure noticed how rarely people openly displayed their military dog tags. He envisioned one day each year when service members, veterans, families, and supporters could wear dog tags proudly and invite the public to listen.

That day is April 18: Dog Tag Day.

The Dog Tag Day pledge

I will wear, honor, listen, and remember.

I pledge to honor those who served, respect the stories carried by their dog tags, listen with gratitude, and help keep their service and sacrifice alive for future generations.
