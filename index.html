Our Mission

Don’t let our veterans fade into the background.

Dog Tag Day is a national movement built around one simple act of visibility. On April 18, veterans and service members are invited to wear or display their dog tags so America can see the people behind the service. Families may wear tags in honor or memory of someone they love.

Veterans Day and Memorial Day are sacred American observances. Dog Tag Day does not replace them. April 18 gives us another way to recognize the millions of people who raised their right hand, served this country, sacrificed time with family, worked for little pay, and carried the responsibilities of military service.

Identity • Brotherhood • Honor. Those stories deserve to be seen, heard, and remembered.

One Day. One Visible Sign.

April 18 — What Do I Do?

If you served: Put on your dog tags. They say, “I served.”

If you see the tags: Acknowledge the person wearing them — a nod, handshake, fist to the heart, or simply, “I see you.”

If you are family: Wear or display the tags of someone you love and help keep their story alive.

Take a picture or video. Share the person, the tags, and the story behind them.

What the Tags Mean

More than metal. A life of service.

Dog tags are one of the most recognizable symbols of military identity. For Dog Tag Day, they become a visible connection between those who served and the communities they served.

From the Founder

I See Your Dog Tags

When I see your dog tags, I know something about you. I know what you went through to earn them.

It doesn’t matter what job we did in the military, what rank we reached, or what direction our service took us. We all started in the same place — as recruits.

I still remember receiving my first dog tags. To me, they were my first piece of jewelry. They had my name on them. They were mine. I belonged to something bigger than myself.

That feeling never left me.

So when I see another veteran wearing those tags, I don’t need to know their rank or their whole story before I acknowledge them.

I see your dog tags.
I know you served.
I know where you started.
And I see you.
