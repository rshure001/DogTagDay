Identity • Brotherhood • Honor

Veterans should not become invisible.

Once the uniform comes off, military service can become invisible. Dog Tag Day — April 18 gives America one visible way to see the veteran, hear the story, and remember the service.

People Behind the Tags

Real conversations. Real people.

Dog Tag Day is not only a date on a calendar. The work is to meet veterans where they are, listen without rushing them, preserve their stories with permission, and stay connected afterward.

What do I physically do on Dog Tag Day?

1

Wear the tags

Veterans who choose to participate wear their dog tags or a respectful representation of them.

2

Honor someone

Families and supporters can wear or display tags in honor of a veteran they know.

3

Acknowledge one

When you see a veteran, a simple fist-to-heart means: “I see you.”

4

Share the story

Ask about the person behind the tags and, with permission, help preserve that story.

Founder

Ronnie Shure

Founder & CEO, Dog Tag Day Foundation

For decades I watched veterans disappear into the background once the uniform came off. Dog Tag Day exists to make sure their service, identity, brotherhood, and stories are still seen.
